01
A claim is a command, not a comment
On GitHub, "tests pass" is a sentence. Here it is a command the forge runs again, on its own machine, and reports back on.
02
A merge is a decision, not a button
Nothing lands because someone had permission and felt good about it. It lands because the repository’s rules were met, and the receipt lists which.
03
Agents are staff, not borrowed logins
Their own accounts, their own narrow permissions, their own track record of being right.
04
The review queue is ranked by need
Not newest first. Disputes, split decisions and code resting on nothing but a description — plus a random sample, so nothing ships entirely unread.

Still don't get it?

Fine. Pick a story. No computer words, promise. If a ten-year-old can't follow this, that's on us.

01 The catch

Your duck comes back with a fish and says: "I caught this. It’s safe. Trust me."

02 The double-check

A different duck, who doesn’t care about being the hero, goes and catches the same fish the same way. Same fish? Great. Different fish? Nobody eats.

03 The sign

The pond has a sign with the rules: a grown-up looks at the fish, two ducks agree, nobody says "wait". Until the sign is happy, the fish stays in the bucket.

04 The tag

When you finally eat, a little tag stays on the plate forever: who caught it, who checked it, who said okay. Years later anyone can read the tag.

01 The batch

Your little brother makes a jug of lemonade and says: "I tasted it. It’s good."

02 The taste test

Your friend tastes it too, without being told what your brother said. If your friend makes a face, that jug never reaches the stand.

03 Mom’s rule

Someone who didn’t make it tastes it, the price on the sign is right, and nobody is allergic. All three, or it stays in the kitchen.

04 The sticker

Every cup that sells gets a sticker: who made it, who tasted it, who said okay. The stickers never come off.

01 The homework

Your robot does your math homework and says: "Done. I checked it."

02 The second robot

A second robot does the same problems from scratch without peeking. If the answers don’t match, the homework doesn’t leave the house.

03 Dad’s rule

Matching answers, a human reads it, no skipped questions. Dad doesn’t argue about it; it’s written on the fridge.

04 The back page

Stapled to the back: who did it, who re-did it, who read it, and which fridge rule let it go to school.

That's the whole product. Everything else on this page is this story with worse words.

If someone gets a bellyache, you know exactly which cup, and who tasted it first.

Your teacher can read the back page. So can you, in June, when you’ve forgotten everything.

Straight answers. Including the ones that aren't flattering.

One extra step: saying what you ran. Everything after that is automatic, and it replaces the ritual of pressing approve on a diff you skimmed. Cloning, pushing and branching are identical.

Probably, eventually, without meaning to. That is the entire premise. Claims are re-run by something that has no stake in the outcome, and a disagreement blocks the change instead of becoming a green check.

They need to know one thing: push to refs/for/main instead of main. No new client, no desktop app, no plugin. If you forget, the forge tells you in the push output.

It slows down the part where unverified code reaches main, on purpose. Everything else runs on your own CI at your own speed, and changes that meet the rules land without waiting for a human to notice.

It’s git. Clone it and you have everything, receipts included — they live on the commits as git notes, not in our database. No export flow to request, no sales call to sit through.
